I: Thank you for joining. Could you describe how nights are staffed where you work?
P02: It's a nursing home. There are twenty residents on my floor, and at night it's just me. There's one other person on the floor next door, so if something happens I call them on the internal phone.
I: Twenty residents on your own.
P02: Yes. I'm used to it now, but at first it was frightening. If two call bells go off at once, you have to decide which one to go to first. On your own.
I: How do you make that decision?
P02: Experience... and gut feeling. But I always think, what if someone falls in the room I didn't go to? At night there's nobody to ask.
I: Nobody to ask.
P02: The nurse is on call, so I can phone. But it feels wrong to wake them in the middle of the night. So first you agonise over whether to call at all.
P02: In the end I mostly carry it myself until the morning.
I: How do you spend the time after a night like that?
P02: I drive home in a daze. Even when I get in, I can't sleep straight away. My head is too wired. Next thing I know I'm watching daytime TV.
I: Unable to sleep.
P02: Then I fall asleep in the evening, wake up at night, and my whole routine is a mess (laughs). I can't make plans with friends until the rota comes out, so gradually they stopped asking.
I: They stopped asking.
P02: It can't be helped. I'm working while everyone else is asleep. It feels like I'm living in a different time zone from everyone.
I: What about your colleagues?
P02: The person on the next floor and I text each other in the middle of the night — "still alive?" (laughs). That's all it is, but when a reply comes I feel relieved.
P02: In the morning, when the early shift arrives, they ask "well done, was it a quiet one?" That's when all the tension drains out of me.
I: Are there things you value about working nights?
P02: Hmm... when a resident can't sleep and we sit and chat for a bit, sometimes they show a side of themselves they never show in the day. At those moments I'm glad it's night.
