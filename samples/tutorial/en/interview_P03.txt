I: Thanks for taking part. How do you feel about night shifts?
P03: Actually, I prefer nights. People tell me I'm odd.
I: What do you like about them?
P03: It's quiet. In the day there are tests, relatives, doctors' orders coming in one after another, and I never get to properly talk to patients. At night I can care for them at my own pace.
I: At your own pace.
P03: Yes. At night I feel trusted with things. Of course the responsibility is heavy, but I can think for myself and act. I think that suits me.
I: Are there times when you feel the weight of that responsibility?
P03: Definitely. In my first year, a patient's breathing changed in the early hours. My senior was in another room and I had to phone the doctor myself. My hands were shaking.
P03: I remember thinking, "at night nobody is going to help you". Looking back, my senior came straight away.
I: How do you feel about it now?
P03: I think that experience is what taught me to make decisions. Nights brought me up, in a way (laughs).
I: How about physically?
P03: I'm still young, so I'm fine... is what I'd like to say. But the day after a night is a write-off. I wake up and it's evening and I think, "what did I even do today?"
I: And balancing it with your private life?
P03: I live alone, so it's not too bad. But my parents ask "are you sleeping properly?" every single time (laughs). Just hearing "night shift" makes people worry.
I: How about your colleagues?
P03: The senior nurses on nights have been so good to me. On breaks they teach me things I could never ask in the day. I actually get to talk to my seniors at night.
I: More than in the day.
P03: In the day everyone is too busy. Talking in the break room at night over instant noodles — I think that's really important time.
