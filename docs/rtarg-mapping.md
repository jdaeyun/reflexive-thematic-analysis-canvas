# Thematic Analysis Canvas and the RTARG

This page maps the **Reflexive Thematic Analysis Reporting Guidelines (RTARG)** to what Thematic Analysis Canvas does. It shows where the app supports good reporting, where reporting is the researcher's job, and where the app still conflicts with the guidelines.

> Braun, V., & Clarke, V. (2024). Supporting best practice in reflexive thematic analysis reporting in *Palliative Medicine*: A review of published research and introduction to the Reflexive Thematic Analysis Reporting Guidelines (RTARG). *Palliative Medicine, 38*(6), 608–616. https://doi.org/10.1177/02692163241234800 — The RTARG is in the article's Supplemental Material. The article is published under CC BY 4.0.

Notes on this mapping:

* RTARG items are **paraphrased**. Read the original before reporting. Item numbers are ours: the RTARG lists its items under headings without numbering them.
* The RTARG offers **guidelines, not a checklist**. Not every item applies to every study, and "unthinking compliance" does not improve quality. The same holds for this app: it can support reflexive TA, but it cannot make an analysis reflexive.
* For each item the RTARG also lists **practices to avoid**. The last column notes when the app could push towards one of them.

**Status:**

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ✅ | Supported: the app does this, or produces the material for it |
| 🟡 | Partly supported, or supported indirectly |
| ✍️ | The researcher's writing or design decision; outside the app |
| ⬜ | Not supported yet, but the app could help |
| ⚠️ | Conflict: the app currently does something the RTARG advises against |

## The Introduction

| # | RTARG advice (paraphrased) | Status | In the app / what to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Give a robust context and rationale; enter a conversation with existing scholarship (not "finding a gap") | ✍️ | — |
| 2 | State a methodologically coherent research question (not a hypothesis) | 🟡 | *Project setup → Research question*; shown in the Analysis summary. Wording is up to the researcher. |
| 3 | Own your perspectives: state guiding theoretical assumptions (paradigm, ontology, epistemology) and other theory | ⬜ | No field yet. Planned: optional fields in Project setup, carried into the Analysis summary. |
| 4 | Report consistently with those assumptions throughout (no "accurate / reliable" interpretation) | 🟡 | The app's own wording avoids accuracy and reliability language, and themes are "generated", not "found". The researcher's text is their own. |
| 5 | Show methodological coherence between assumptions, questions, data generation and RTA | 🟡 | The app supports reflexive TA only: no codebook mode, no agreement statistics. Coherence of the study as a whole is the researcher's. |
| 6 | Show evidence of reflexive practice (positioning, role, reflexive journaling) | ✅ | Reflexive journal (Ctrl/⌘+J from anywhere; types include *Positionality*), optional "Why?" with decisions, snapshots of the theme structure, Appendix A. |
| 7 | Write in a first-person style | ✍️ | The narrative editor does not enforce a style. A first-person prompt could be added to its hints. |

## The Methodology

| # | RTARG advice (paraphrased) | Status | In the app / what to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | Describe how participants or data items were selected (avoid "sample/sampling") | ✍️ | The app does not use "sample". |
| 9 | Give the number of participants or data items and a rationale (e.g. information power; not saturation) | 🟡 | The Analysis summary counts documents and participants (interviewers excluded). The rationale is the researcher's. The app never mentions saturation. |
| 10 | Describe participant characteristics, balancing context with anonymity (aggregate or minimal demographics) | ⚠️ | **Conflict.** Table 1 lists each participant's attributes line by line, which the RTARG names as a practice to avoid. To fix: summarise attributes in aggregate by default, and make the per-participant table an explicit opt-in with an anonymity warning. |
| 11 | Report ethics approval, principles, informed consent; do not compromise anonymity | ✍️ | Data stays in the browser; pseudonymous participant labels are supported. Reporting is the researcher's. |
| 12 | Give a rationale for the method(s) of data generation (avoid triangulation as rationale) | ✍️ | — |
| 13 | Describe the data generation tool(s); put them in supplementary materials where possible | ✍️ | — |
| 14 | Give modality, setting, time frame and recording (without treating non-standardisation as bias) | ✍️ | — |
| 15 | Describe who generated interactive data, and their positioning and relationship with participants | 🟡 | Interviewers can be tagged as such. Disclosure and positioning go in the journal (*Positionality*) and the report. |
| 16 | Describe the size and scope of the dataset (e.g. range and average length of interviews or texts) | 🟡 | Each document's length is recorded at import (audit trail). To add: range and mean length in the Analysis summary, without implying that more data means better data. |
| 17 | Describe data preparation: transcription, corrections, removal of identifying details, pseudonyms | 🟡 | Imported text is kept exactly as imported, and the encoding is logged. There is no field for transcription conventions or de-identification yet. These could be a familiarisation or journal note, or a Project setup field. |
| 18 | Give a rationale for using RTA; justify any combination with other approaches (e.g. a codebook) | 🟡 | The app is RTA-only and offers no codebook. The code list is an analytic record and is left out of exports by default. The rationale is the researcher's. |
| 19 | Describe the orientation to RTA (inductive ↔ deductive, semantic ↔ latent) | ⬜ | No field yet. Planned together with item 3. |
| 20 | Give a specific, situated account of the analytic process, not a generic list of the six phases (use supplementary materials) | ✅ | The audit trail, journal, decision reasons and "Structure then" snapshots record the actual process. Appendix A exports it as supplementary material. The app never prints a generic six-phase description. |
| 21 | If more than one person analysed: describe roles, how collaborative coding worked and how differences were handled (no inter-coder agreement or consensus coding) | 🟡 | The researcher name is recorded with every action and journal entry. There are no agreement measures, by design. Team meetings can be logged (*Supervisor / team meeting*). Merging two analysts' projects is not supported. |
| 22 | Use language that conveys the researcher's active role ("generating", "developing" themes; not "emerging", "found", "identified") | ✅ | The app's wording follows this in English and Japanese, e.g. "Generating initial themes" and "candidate theme". |

## The Analysis

The RTARG prefers the heading **Analysis** over *Results* or *Findings*. The app's exported write-up uses "Analysis" / 「分析」.

| # | RTARG advice (paraphrased) | Status | In the app / what to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 23 | Give an overview of the themes or thematic structure (list, map or table) | 🟡 | The "Themes and illustrative extracts" table and the narrative's headings give the structure. A thematic map is not available yet. |
| 24 | Conceptualise themes as shared meaning around a central organising concept (not topic summaries) | ✅ | The Phase 5 definition field asks for the central organising concept. The empty theme board explains that themes are patterns of shared meaning, not topic summaries. |
| 25 | Name themes so they capture the essence or story (not one-word topic labels) | 🟡 | Free naming, renames are logged. A gentle hint for very short names could be added. |
| 26 | Report themes in depth: multifaceted, with data and analytic narrative; not too many themes | 🟡 | A narrative editor for every theme. No warning about the number of themes. |
| 27 | Use subthemes judiciously; avoid a fragmented, "bitty" structure | 🟡 | Only one level of subthemes is possible. No further guidance. |
| 28 | Make the analytic narrative explain meaning and significance; no frequency counts as justification; do not let data "speak for themselves" | ✅ | Phase 6 opens on the narrative editor. Prevalence counts are hidden by default and left out of exports unless chosen. |
| 29 | Balance analytic narrative and data extracts drawn from across the dataset | ✅ | The theme's extracts sit next to the narrative and can be inserted at the cursor. The table's illustrative extracts are spread across participants, with key extracts first. |
| 30 | Make narrative and extracts cohere; consider alternative readings | ✍️ | Every extract links back to its place in the source, which helps when re-reading in context. |
| 31 | Integrate existing research and theory into the analytic narrative (not a separate Results/Discussion split) | 🟡 | The narrative is free text, so theory can be woven in. The editor hints could say so explicitly. |

## The final section (General discussion, Conclusions, Implications…)

| # | RTARG advice (paraphrased) | Status | In the app / what to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 32 | Draw analytic conclusions across themes: the "so what" of the overall story | 🟡 | The *Overview of the analysis* field in the narrative editor is meant for the overall story. A separate conclusions field could be added. |
| 33 | Give implications and directions for future research that follow from the analysis | ✍️ | — |
| 34 | Use quality practices coherent with RTA (reflexive journaling, critical friends, member reflections; not member checking, triangulation or theme agreement) | ✅ | Reflexive journal and team / supervisor meeting entries. There is nothing for agreement or corroboration of themes, by design. |
| 35 | Evaluate the research from a Big Q standpoint (situatedness is not a limitation) | ✍️ | — |
| 36 | Reflect on the research process and the researcher's role in shaping the knowledge | ✅ | Reflexive journal, decision reasons and Appendix A. |

## Summary

* **Supported (✅):** reflexive practice and the account of the analytic process (6, 20, 34, 36), coherent language (22), theme conceptualisation (24), and an analytic narrative that does not rest on frequency (28, 29).
* **Conflict to fix (⚠️):** item 10, the per-participant characteristics table.
* **Next steps the app could take (⬜ / 🟡):**
  * Optional Project setup fields for theoretical assumptions and orientation (3, 19), and for data preparation (17)
  * Dataset length range and mean in the Analysis summary (16)
  * A thematic map (23)
  * Writing hints for theme names, subthemes, first person and integrating theory (7, 25, 27, 31)
  * A conclusions field (32)
* **The researcher's work (✍️):** design, ethics, data generation and the discussion. The app records and organises the analysis; it does not write the methodology for you.
