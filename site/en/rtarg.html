RTARG mapping

How the app relates to each item of the Reflexive Thematic Analysis Reporting Guidelines. Use it as a check when you write up. An independent project, not affiliated with or endorsed by Virginia Braun or Victoria Clarke.

This page maps the Reflexive Thematic Analysis Reporting Guidelines (RTARG) to what Reflexive Thematic Analysis Canvas does. It shows where the app supports good reporting, where reporting is the researcher's job, and where the app still conflicts with the guidelines.

Braun, V., & Clarke, V. (2024). Supporting best practice in reflexive thematic analysis reporting in Palliative Medicine: A review of published research and introduction to the Reflexive Thematic Analysis Reporting Guidelines (RTARG). Palliative Medicine, 38(6), 608–616. https://doi.org/10.1177/02692163241234800 — The RTARG is in the article's Supplemental Material. The article is published under CC BY 4.0.

Notes on this mapping:

  • RTARG items are paraphrased. Read the original before reporting. Item numbers are ours: the RTARG lists its items under headings without numbering them.
  • The RTARG offers guidelines, not a checklist. Not every item applies to every study, and "unthinking compliance" does not improve quality. The same holds for this app: it can support reflexive TA, but it cannot make an analysis reflexive.
  • For each item the RTARG also lists practices to avoid. The last column notes when the app could push towards one of them.

The Introduction

#RTARG advice (paraphrased)StatusIn the app / what to do
1Give a robust context and rationale; enter a conversation with existing scholarship (not "finding a gap")✍️—
2State a methodologically coherent research question (not a hypothesis)🟡Project setup → Research question; shown in the Analysis summary. Wording is up to the researcher.
3Own your perspectives: state guiding theoretical assumptions (paradigm, ontology, epistemology) and other theory✅Project setup → Positioning and data preparation → Theoretical assumptions and theory (optional). Shown in the Analysis summary; changes are logged with before / after.
4Report consistently with those assumptions throughout (no "accurate / reliable" interpretation)🟡The app's own wording avoids accuracy and reliability language, and themes are "generated", not "found". The researcher's text is their own.
5Show methodological coherence between assumptions, questions, data generation and RTA🟡The app supports reflexive TA only: no codebook mode, no agreement statistics. Coherence of the study as a whole is the researcher's.
6Show evidence of reflexive practice (positioning, role, reflexive journaling)✅Reflexive journal (Ctrl/⌘+J from anywhere; types include Positionality), optional "Why?" with decisions, snapshots of the theme structure, Appendix A.
7Write in a first-person style🟡The narrative editor's guidance asks for the first person ("you made these themes"). The style is the researcher's.

The Methodology

#RTARG advice (paraphrased)StatusIn the app / what to do
8Describe how participants or data items were selected (avoid "sample/sampling")✍️The app does not use "sample".
9Give the number of participants or data items and a rationale (e.g. information power; not saturation)🟡The Analysis summary counts documents and participants (interviewers excluded). The rationale is the researcher's. The app never mentions saturation.
10Describe participant characteristics, balancing context with anonymity (aggregate or minimal demographics)✅Table 1 summarises the participant group by default (each attribute with the number of participants per value, interviewers excluded). Values that only one participant has are pointed out in the preview (never exported). Listing participants one by one is an explicit option with an anonymity warning.
11Report ethics approval, principles, informed consent; do not compromise anonymity✍️Data stays in the browser; pseudonymous participant labels are supported. Reporting is the researcher's.
12Give a rationale for the method(s) of data generation (avoid triangulation as rationale)✍️—
13Describe the data generation tool(s); put them in supplementary materials where possible✍️—
14Give modality, setting, time frame and recording (without treating non-standardisation as bias)✍️—
15Describe who generated interactive data, and their positioning and relationship with participants🟡Interviewers can be tagged as such. Disclosure and positioning go in the journal (Positionality) and the report.
16Describe the size and scope of the dataset (e.g. range and average length of interviews or texts)✅The Analysis summary gives the range and mean length of documents and of survey answers: characters, plus words for space-delimited text. The note says length describes the dataset and is not a marker of quality.
17Describe data preparation: transcription, corrections, removal of identifying details, pseudonyms✅Project setup → Data preparation (optional), shown in the Analysis summary. Imported text is kept exactly as imported, and the encoding is logged.
18Give a rationale for using RTA; justify any combination with other approaches (e.g. a codebook)🟡The app is RTA-only and offers no codebook. The code list is an analytic record and is left out of exports by default. The rationale is the researcher's.
19Describe the orientation to RTA (inductive ↔ deductive, semantic ↔ latent)✅Project setup has a five-point choice for each dimension (optional), shown in the Analysis summary. Changes are logged.
20Give a specific, situated account of the analytic process, not a generic list of the six phases (use supplementary materials)✅The audit trail, journal, decision reasons and "Structure then" snapshots record the actual process. Appendix A exports it as supplementary material. The app never prints a generic six-phase description.
21If more than one person analysed: describe roles, how collaborative coding worked and how differences were handled (no inter-coder agreement or consensus coding)🟡Every action and journal entry records the researcher's name. Journal entries of the type Supervisor / team meeting start with an outline for this item: who took part, where readings differed, what each rested on, how it was handled, and what changed. There are no agreement measures and no merging of projects, by design; the guide describes how to work as a team.
22Use language that conveys the researcher's active role ("generating", "developing" themes; not "emerging", "found", "identified")✅The app's wording follows this in English and Japanese, e.g. "Generating initial themes" and "candidate theme".

The Analysis

The RTARG prefers the heading Analysis over Results or Findings. The app's exported write-up uses "Analysis" / 「分析」.

#RTARG advice (paraphrased)StatusIn the app / what to do
23Give an overview of the themes or thematic structure (list, map or table)✅Themes → Map: a thematic map with themes, subthemes and named relationships, saved as .svg and placed in the .docx write-up as Figure 1. The themes table and the narrative headings give the structure as well.
24Conceptualise themes as shared meaning around a central organising concept (not topic summaries)✅The Phase 5 definition field asks for the central organising concept. The empty theme board explains that themes are patterns of shared meaning, not topic summaries.
25Name themes so they capture the essence or story (not one-word topic labels)🟡The phase 5 section asks whether the name tells the theme's story rather than only naming a topic (a short quote can work). No length rule is applied. Renames are logged.
26Report themes in depth: multifaceted, with data and analytic narrative; not too many themes🟡A narrative editor for every theme. Once reviewing has begun, the board asks whether each theme has enough depth and whether some could be combined or set aside — a question, not a maximum number.
27Use subthemes judiciously; avoid a fragmented, "bitty" structure🟡Only one level of subthemes is possible. A theme with subthemes asks whether each shows an important facet of the central concept (no count threshold).
28Make the analytic narrative explain meaning and significance; no frequency counts as justification; do not let data "speak for themselves"✅Phase 6 opens on the narrative editor. Prevalence counts are hidden by default and left out of exports unless chosen.
29Balance analytic narrative and data extracts drawn from across the dataset✅The theme's extracts sit next to the narrative and can be inserted at the cursor. The table's illustrative extracts are the key extracts the researcher marked (★): choosing them is an analytic decision, so none are filled in automatically.
30Make narrative and extracts cohere; consider alternative readings✍️Every extract links back to its place in the source, which helps when re-reading in context. Full-text search across all documents helps to look for talk that complicates a theme, and the extract filter lets you read a code's extracts group by group (for reading only: counts and exports are unchanged).
31Integrate existing research and theory into the analytic narrative (not a separate Results/Discussion split)🟡The narrative editor's guidance asks to weave research and theory into each theme rather than save them for a separate discussion. The writing is the researcher's.

The final section (General discussion, Conclusions, Implications…)

#RTARG advice (paraphrased)StatusIn the app / what to do
32Draw analytic conclusions across themes: the "so what" of the overall story✅A General discussion field after the themes in the narrative editor (exported under that heading), with guidance not to repeat the themes one by one.
33Give implications and directions for future research that follow from the analysis✍️—
34Use quality practices coherent with RTA (reflexive journaling, critical friends, member reflections; not member checking, triangulation or theme agreement)✅Reflexive journal and team / supervisor meeting entries. There is nothing for agreement or corroboration of themes, by design.
35Evaluate the research from a Big Q standpoint (situatedness is not a limitation)✍️—
36Reflect on the research process and the researcher's role in shaping the knowledge✅Reflexive journal, decision reasons and Appendix A.

Summary

  • Supported (✅):
    • Positioning: theoretical assumptions, orientation and data preparation (3, 17, 19)
    • The dataset: length of documents and answers (16), and the participant group summarised to protect anonymity (10)
    • The process: reflexive practice and the account of the analytic process (6, 20, 34, 36), and coherent language (22)
    • The analysis: theme conceptualisation (24), a thematic map (23), an analytic narrative that does not rest on frequency (28, 29), and a general discussion across themes (32)
  • Conflicts: none left. Table 1 now summarises the group by default (10).
  • Supported indirectly (🟡): gentle, non-blocking hints for theme names, subthemes, the number of themes, first-person writing and integrating theory (7, 25, 26, 27, 31). The app can prompt, but these remain writing decisions.
  • The researcher's work (✍️): design, ethics, data generation and the discussion. The app records and organises the analysis; it does not write the methodology for you.
